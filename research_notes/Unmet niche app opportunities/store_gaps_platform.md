# App-Store Gaps and New Platform Capabilities (2025–2026): Unmet App Opportunities for a Solo Developer

**How the store checks were done (read first):** On 2026-10-14 I scraped the US App Store web search (`apps.apple.com/us/iphone/search?term=…`) and US Google Play search (`play.google.com/store/search?q=…&c=apps`) for about 65 keywords. For each keyword I opened the top 8 listings and recorded the rating, the rating count, the date of the latest version (from version history), the price, and the developer. "Ratings" means the US-storefront rating count shown on the listing. On Google Play I also recorded the download band. The raw output is saved in the session scratchpad (`sgscripts/out*`, `final/`). **Limitation:** I could not get keyword *search-volume / popularity* scores. Apple Search Ads popularity, Appfigures, Sensor Tower and AppTweak are paywalled, and appfigures.com was not reachable from this environment. Demand is therefore inferred from three proxies: (a) participation and population statistics, (b) download bands of the leading app for the same need on Google Play, and (c) rating counts of abandoned incumbents. The report writer should present demand as *proxied*, not as measured keyword volume.

## Q1. Which App Store / Google Play niches have real demand but weak competition in 2025–2026?

### Takeaway
By October 2026 the old pattern ("a high-demand keyword with no apps") is mostly gone. AI-assisted building has flooded the stores, and most "empty" niches now have 5–8 apps that launched in 2025–26 with 0–20 ratings each. The real gaps are now (1) **cross-platform gaps**, where Android has a 100K–1M+ download leader and iOS has nothing with more than about 120 ratings; (2) **abandoned leaders**, where the top result has hundreds or thousands of ratings but has not been updated in 2–10 years; and (3) **fragmented niches with large real-world populations** and no trusted winner. I verified 11 such niches against both stores (A1–A11 below). Many tempting niches turned out to be well served and are listed as "screened out".

### Inferences
- **Feasibility ranking for a solo dev with an AI coding assistant and a small budget, from my synthesis:**
  1. **A1 iOS/iPad cut-list optimizer.** Strongest proof of demand (1M+ Android). Fully offline algorithmic app (2D guillotine bin-packing, PDF/label export), no backend, and a natural fit for one-time purchase or pro unlock. The risk is a long tail of 2026 clones, so the moat is quality, an iPad layout and Apple Pencil sketch input.
  2. **A2 iOS commute "wake me at my stop" alarm.** Android proves demand (100K+). The iOS field is abandoned. AlarmKit finally lets a third-party alarm break through silent mode and Focus. **Key technical risk, to prototype first:** AlarmKit is time-based. The app would need a background geofence callback to schedule an immediate countdown alarm, or to keep re-scheduling an ETA-based alarm. I found no documentation that confirms or rules this out.
  3. **A4 pickleball scorer.** Large participation (24.3M) and no scorekeeper above 88 ratings. Apple Watch-first scoring, a Live Activity, and voice ("Siri, point for us") via App Intents are all solo-feasible. Monetize via a lifetime unlock plus Watch faces. DUPR result submission would need partner access (not verified).
  4. **A3 iOS POTA/SOTA logger.** A passionate, tool-buying niche with stale iOS incumbents. Needs ADIF export, POTA/SOTA spot feeds and callsign lookup. The market is small (about 30K activators) and US licenses are declining, so it suits a single lifetime-price app rather than a business.
  5. **A8 homeschool records.** Growing population with weak apps on both stores. The differentiator is state-specific compliance logs (attendance days, hours) and portfolio PDF export. On-device summarization of work samples (Foundation Models) is a cheap add-on.
- **Medium or lower priority:** A5 backyard chickens (real audience, but demand evidence is from early 2025, and low willingness to pay is likely). A6 volunteer hours (clear abandoned leader, but a low revenue ceiling; could be a free app with a school upsell). A7 baby sign (needs filmed sign videos, so content cost and Deaf-community collaboration are essential). A9 warranty vault (no winner, but flooded; distribution is the problem). A10 AAC (strong price gap, but high stakes for vulnerable users, symbol-set licensing questions, and clinician/SLP trust needed; feasible only with careful scoping). A11 caregiver coordination (huge demand, but needs multi-user sync and trust; a land-grab of 2026 entrants is under way).
- **"Gap" now means "no trusted winner", not "no app".** In 2026 a new entrant must expect 5–10 vibe-coded rivals in any visible niche. The defensible angles are cross-platform gaps (porting a proven Android concept to iOS, or the reverse), deep platform integration (Watch, Live Activities, AlarmKit, Siri/App Intents), and domain depth that a weekend clone will not have (ADIF/POTA rules, state homeschool law, woodworking kerf/grain constraints).

### Gaps
- No keyword popularity or search-volume data (Apple Search Ads popularity, Appfigures, AppTweak or Sensor Tower). Demand rests on participation statistics and Android download bands.
- Rating counts are US-only and do not reflect revenue. I did not check non-US storefronts, where some gaps may differ (for example, ham radio and pickleball in Europe).
- The scrape covers only the top 8 search results per keyword. Strong apps that rank under other keywords may be missed. For example, the climbing apps KAYA and Vertical-Life did not appear for "climbing log", so climbing was left out of the list.
- I did not verify web or desktop competitors (Homeschool Planet, CaringBridge, Lotsa Helping Hands, desktop cut-list software), which could absorb demand.
- I found no demand statistics for baby sign language, volunteer-hour logging or warranty tracking.
- The large AppOpportunity, BigIdeasDB and NichesHunter niche datasets could not be fetched (DNS failure), so their claims are unverified.

## Q2. Which new platform capabilities (iOS 26/27, Android 16/17) enable apps that few or none have built yet?

### Takeaway
The most important new capability for indie developers is economic, not technical. Since WWDC26 (June 2026), Apple lets Small Business Program developers with fewer than 2M first-time downloads call **Apple Foundation Models on Private Cloud Compute at no API cost**, subject to daily limits. On-device and cloud LLM features therefore no longer force a subscription. Alongside this: **AlarmKit** (iOS 26) allows real third-party alarms; **Visual Intelligence and App Intents** let the system camera and Siri reach into app content; and iOS 27 adds **Foundation Models multimodal input with Vision tools**, the **Music Understanding** framework, **Core AI**, and App Intents **entity schemas and View Annotations**. On Android, Gemini Nano's **Prompt API is still Beta and limited to a short list of flagships**, and **Live Updates** (Android 16) are under-used outside delivery and rideshare apps. I found no adoption counts for any of these APIs.

### Cited Findings

**Apple: iOS 27 / WWDC26 (June 8 2026)**
- The Foundation Models framework now works with "any language model": Apple's on-device model, cloud models such as Claude and Gemini, or any provider that conforms to the Language Model protocol. It adds **multimodal prompts** (images plus text), **Vision tools (OCR, barcode readers) that the model can call on-device**, **Dynamic Profiles** (swap models, tools and instructions mid-session), an **Evaluations framework**, and an `fm` CLI and Python SDK. — [Apple WWDC26 iOS guide](https://developer.apple.com/wwdc26/guides/ios/)
- **Free PCC access:** eligibility requires all three of Small Business Program enrollment, fewer than 2M first-time App Store downloads across the developer's apps, and the PCC entitlement. Access is "at no cloud API cost" but "subject to daily usage limits" that vary by feature, request complexity and system demand. If a developer crosses the threshold, they get 6 months to migrate. "Expanded access will be available for a fee in the future." TestFlight installs don't count toward the threshold. — [Apple: Accessing Private Cloud Compute](https://developer.apple.com/private-cloud-compute); [MacRumors, Jun 9 2026](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/06/09/apple-outlines-major-ai-and-developer-tool-updates/); [TechCrunch, Jun 8 2026](https://techcrunch.com/2026/06/08/apple-bets-cheaper-ai-will-woo-small-developers/)
- **Core AI (new):** a Swift API to "load, specialize, and run AI models entirely on-device" with "zero server dependencies and zero token costs", including ahead-of-time compilation. — [Apple WWDC26 iOS guide](https://developer.apple.com/wwdc26/guides/ios/)
- **App Intents:** entity schemas feed the Spotlight semantic index so Siri can surface app content "with attribution back to your app". Intent schemas let people act on content "with no specific phrases to define". The **View Annotations API** maps on-screen views to entities so users can "reference and act on what's on screen conversationally". A new App Intents Testing framework is included. — [Apple WWDC26 iOS guide](https://developer.apple.com/wwdc26/guides/ios/)
- **Music Understanding framework:** on-device analysis across six dimensions (key, rhythm, structure, pace, instrument activity, loudness). Apple handles the signal processing and models, and it works offline. Final Cut Pro uses it for beat grids and auto-syncing clips. — [WWDC26 session 253](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2026/253); [Apple WWDC26 iOS guide](https://developer.apple.com/wwdc26/guides/ios/)
- **NowPlaying framework** connects playback to the Lock Screen, Control Center, Dynamic Island and CarPlay. WidgetKit widgets can be customized through App Intents. — [Apple WWDC26 iOS guide](https://developer.apple.com/wwdc26/guides/ios/)
- One blog claims apps must adopt the DeclaredAgeRange and PermissionKit APIs by September 2026. This is unverified, secondary and possibly inaccurate. — [lushbinary](https://lushbinary.com/blog/wwdc-2026-announcements-ios-27-siri-developer-guide/)

**Apple: iOS 26 (2025)**
- **AlarmKit:** apps can create alarms (fixed or countdown) that "break through the silent mode and the current focus". Countdown alarms use a Live Activity on the Lock Screen, Dynamic Island and StandBy, and custom actions run via App Intents. — [WWDC25 session 230](https://developer.apple.com/wwdc25/230); [DEV summary](https://dev.to/arshtechpro/wwdc-2025-wake-up-to-the-alarmkit-api-ios-26-4e67)

A developer forum thread reports authorization errors ("com.apple.AlarmKit.Alarm error 1"). — [mjtsai.com roundup, Jun 20 2025](https://mjtsai.com/blog/?p=48163); [Apple Developer Forums](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/818416)
- **Visual Intelligence integration:** the framework passes what Visual Intelligence captured to the app. The app uses App Intents to return matching content, which then appears in the system Visual Intelligence UI. According to a secondary write-up, this works via `IntentValueQuery` and `SemanticContentDescriptor`. — [Apple Visual Intelligence docs](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/visualintelligence.md); [TechCrunch, Jun 9 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/06/09/apple-brings-apple-intelligence-to-the-iphone-screen-at-wwdc-2025); [blakecrosley.com](https://blakecrosley.com/blog/app-intents-2-ios-26-additions)

- Foundation Models require Apple Intelligence-capable hardware (iPhone 15 Pro and later, M1+ iPad/Mac); the model downloads after Apple Intelligence is enabled. (Secondary guide.) — [atelier-socle](https://www.atelier-socle.com/en/articles/foundation-models-api-guide)

**Android 16 / 17 and Gemini Nano**
- **ML Kit GenAI Prompt API:** announced in Alpha (InfoQ dates it to Nov 2025). It sends text and multimodal prompts to on-device Gemini Nano, works offline, and is private. ### Inferences (platform-enabled opportunities, B1–B4; several combine with Q1 gaps)
- **B1. Music practice coach built on Music Understanding (iOS 27).** The app records practice and reports tempo drift against a target BPM, detected key, section structure and loudness dynamics, all on-device and offline. It pairs with a practice log, where the leader Andante has 3,055 ratings and other practice logs have ≤9 ([App Store search: piano practice log](https://apps.apple.com/us/iphone/search?term=piano%20practice%20log)). Because the framework shipped with iOS 27 (Sept 2026), almost no apps can have adopted it yet, which gives a first-mover window. It is solo-feasible because Apple handles the signal processing. Risks: requires iOS 27, and Apple's own apps (GarageBand, Final Cut) may absorb the obvious uses.
- **B2. AlarmKit-plus-Live-Activity utilities, with Android Live Updates parity.** These are the commute "wake me at my stop" alarm (A2) and a parking-meter or EV-charging session timer whose Live Activity also shows on CarPlay (iOS 26) and as an Android 16 Live Update. They fit Google's "ongoing, user-initiated, time-sensitive" criteria. iOS parking-timer apps all have ≤3 ratings, but demand there is unproven.
- **B3. "Do I already own this?" inventory with Visual Intelligence and Foundation Models (moving boxes, tools, collections, home contents).** The app registers an `IntentValueQuery` so the system camera or a screenshot search returns the user's own catalogued items. Foundation Models' multimodal input and Vision OCR/barcode tools (iOS 27) extract model numbers, serials and warranty dates on-device at zero cost. This combines A9 (warranty) with the moving-box gap (iOS ≤7 ratings; Android MOVINGBOXES 10K+). On Android, the Prompt API's officially suggested "receipt OCR + categorize" use is the same pattern, but on few devices.
- **B4. "Subscription-killer" AI utilities, enabled by free PCC.** Incumbents in categories such as identifiers, transcription and AI journaling charge subscriptions to cover cloud inference (for example CoinSnap with 307K ratings, and AIBY's Coin ID). Under 2M downloads, an indie developer can offer comparable LLM features with no per-call cost and sell a one-time purchase. **Caveats:** daily limits are unspecified, Apple says expanded access will cost money, the benefit is iOS-only, and generic LLMs may not match purpose-trained identification models in accuracy. This is best treated as a cost structure for A8/A9/A10/B3, not a standalone app.
- **Cross-cutting:** iOS 27's App Intents entity and intent schemas plus View Annotations give niche apps "free" Siri and Spotlight presence. Examples are voice logging a QSO in a ham logger (A3) or calling the score in pickleball (A4). Small apps that adopt early may get system-level discovery that 2026 clones lack. This is my inference, and the size of the discovery effect is unknown.
- **Android on-device LLM is not yet a mass-market base.** With the Prompt API in Beta and limited to flagships, an Android version of any AI feature needs a cloud or rules fallback. Building iOS first (Foundation Models plus free PCC) is the cheaper path for a solo developer.

### Gaps
- No adoption statistics for AlarmKit, Visual Intelligence integration, Foundation Models, Live Activities on CarPlay or Android Live Updates. "Few apps use X" is inferred, not measured.
- Unknown: whether AlarmKit alarms can be scheduled from a background location callback. This is critical for A2.
- PCC daily usage limits are unspecified. iOS 27 adoption share and Apple Intelligence-capable device share as of Oct 2026 were not found.
- I did not find authoritative iOS 27 release notes on Wallet, watchOS 27, CarPlay or HealthKit changes. The WWDC26 iOS guide does not cover these, and secondary iOS 27 blogs conflict (for example, one claims Siri was rebuilt on Gemini, unverified).
- NFC/UWB: I found no 2025–26 change that clearly opens new indie-feasible app types without hardware partnerships or commercial agreements. This was not researched in depth.
- I did not research the SpeechAnalyzer (iOS 26) transcription API specifics. The App Store "transcribe audio offline" search shows only small 2026 entrants (≤87 ratings — [search](https://apps.apple.com/us/iphone/search?term=transcribe%20audio%20offline)), but Apple's built-in transcription likely limits that gap (unverified).

## Q3. Which indie apps launched 2025–2026 found fast traction, and what pattern does that suggest?

### Takeaway
Public, verifiable 2025–26 indie traction data is scarce and mostly self-reported. The documented cases fall into two groups: rare fast hits driven by the founder's own social distribution and a sharp emotional hook (Brainrot), and slow compounding in narrow utilities (Habit Pixel, HabitKit, multi-app portfolios). Base rates are harsh. Most new subscription apps earn under ~$50–$72 a month after a year, and only 4.6% reach $10K a month within two years. I found no public revenue case for an indie app built on iOS 26/27 APIs.

### Cited Findings
- **Brainrot** (screen-time app that visualizes "brain rot"): the founder says it made **$26K within 30 days** of launch. It was built in Swift with Superwall and PostHog by a first-time mobile developer, was rejected 6 times by App Review, and credits growth to the founder's own social following. — [We Are Founders](https://www.wearefounders.uk/he-built-an-app-to-fix-his-phone-addiction-it-made-26k-in-30-days/); [Indie Hackers post](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/fYbp8x4WWqltdifUwCZJ). **Conflict:** Tiny Startups reported $10.1K/month and "over $26,000" since an end-of-May launch, which suggests $26K may be cumulative rather than the first month. — [Tiny Startups](https://tinystartups.beehiiv.com/p/twits-71). Appfigures commentary cites the same app as evidence that subscriptions plus creator-led TikTok/Instagram growth work for small teams (via search summary). — [Appfigures This Week in Apps](https://appfigures.com/resources/this-week-in-apps/20260213)
- **Habit Pixel** (habit tracker, solo developer): launched May 2025, reached ~$1K MRR in 8 months with ~900 active subscribers, helped by a Black Friday lifetime deal and an Uneed launch. — [Indie Hackers, Jan 2026](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/from-0-to-1k-mrr-in-8-months-bootstrapping-habit-pixel-as-a-solo-dev-684b6c056d)
- **HabitKit:** reported at $28K MRR (Jan 2026) after ~4 years of public, compounding work by a solo developer. This is a secondary source. — [theswiftk.it.com](https://theswiftk.it.com/blog/zero-to-10k-mrr-indie-ios-app)
- **Portfolio model:** one developer went from a failed app to a 30-app portfolio earning ~$22K/month in under a year, about $730/app on average. — [Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/tech/from-failed-app-to-30-app-portfolio-making-22k-mo-in-less-than-a-year-myy3U7K9evxGOVOHti8s). Another rebuilt a portfolio to $60K/month after Apple froze his account. — [Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/tech/building-an-app-portfolio-to-60k-mo-after-apple-froze-his-developer-account-LD7oNYzKSmWucRfKV1AO)
- **Base rates:** RevenueCat 2026 says 4.6% of new apps reach $10K/month within two years, and apps launched before 2020 hold 69% of subscription revenue. — [RevenueCat](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps); [Subscription Insider](https://www.subscriptioninsider.com/article-type/news/revenuecat-data-shows-subscription-app-growth-concentrating-at-the-top). Secondary citations of the report give a median monthly revenue under $50 at 12 months, 17.2% reaching $1K/month and 3.5% reaching $10K. — [theswiftk.it.com](https://theswiftk.it.com/blog/zero-to-10k-mrr-indie-ios-app). Another gives a one-year median of ~$72/month, with the top 10% above $2,574. — [DEV Community](https://dev.to/axrisi/app-economy-2026-why-new-apps-get-3-of-subscription-revenue-3cj8). The figures differ by source and version, so treat them as rough.
- RevenueCat-derived summary: AI apps earn ~41% more per user but churn ~30% faster. (Headline only; body not verified.) — [ppc.land](https://ppc.land/ai-apps-earn-41-more-per-user-but-churn-30-faster-revenuecat-finds/)
- Observed in my scrape: hobby and craft niches can support large audiences for newer apps. Crochet apps YarnPal (33,974 ratings) and Loopsy (16,581) both shipped updates in Oct 2026 and lead "crochet pattern" searches over the older Ribblr (548). — [App Store search: crochet pattern](https://apps.apple.com/us/iphone/search?term=crochet%20pattern). Launch dates and revenue were not verified.

### Inferences
- **Pattern 1: distribution beats the idea.** The fastest documented hit (Brainrot) came from founder-owned social reach plus a shareable visual concept. The gap niches above (woodworking, ham radio, pickleball, homeschool, backyard chickens) all have strong community channels: subreddits, YouTube makers, POTA spotting communities, local pickleball clubs, and homeschool co-ops and Facebook groups. That favors a founder who can show up in those communities.
- **Pattern 2: slow compounding in focused utilities is the median good outcome** (about $1K MRR in 8 months, or $28K MRR after 4 years). Plan for a 12–24 month horizon, or a portfolio of several gap apps (about $700/app/month was a realistic average in the portfolio case).
- **Pattern 3: in 2026 the advantage goes to apps that are hard to vibe-code.** These have domain rules, platform integrations (Watch, AlarmKit, Live Activities, Siri/App Intents, Visual Intelligence) and polish. With release volume up 60–104% and downloads up only ~2%, a generic "AI tracker for X" will be one of many clones.
- **Pattern 4: free PCC changes the pricing.** A solo developer can now offer AI features with one-time or lifetime pricing (as Habit Pixel did with its lifetime deal). Incumbents' subscriptions may then look overpriced in categories where AI costs used to justify recurring fees. This is an inference, untested in public data.

### Gaps
- I found no verified revenue or download figures for any indie app launched in 2025–26 on AlarmKit, Foundation Models, Visual Intelligence or Live Updates.
- I could not verify Brainrot's exact timeline ($26K in the first 30 days versus cumulative) or launch year beyond the sources above.
- I could not determine launch dates or rating velocity for the fast-growing hobby apps (YarnPal, Loopsy, ASL Bloom). Store pages show only recent version history.
- X/Twitter revenue posts and r/iOSProgramming / r/SideProject threads were not retrievable through search in this session. Reddit searches returned only aggregator pages ([GummySearch r/iOSProgramming](https://gummysearch.com/r/iOSProgramming)).
